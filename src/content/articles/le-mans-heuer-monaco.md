In *Le Mans* (1971) Steve McQueen plays Michael Delaney, a driver obsessed with the 24-hour race. The film has very little dialogue and a lot of engine noise, but there is one detail that has outlived almost everything else on screen: the square watch on McQueen's wrist.

## A watch nobody asked for

Heuer launched the Monaco in 1969, alongside one of the first automatic chronograph movements, the Calibre 11. The square, water-resistant case was a bold idea at the time, and sales were modest.

## Why McQueen wore it

McQueen modelled Delaney's look on real drivers of the period, among them Jo Siffert, who raced with Heuer branding on his suit. The racing suit, the patch and the watch came as a set.

> The watch was a costume choice before it became an icon.

## What happened next

Decades later, the Monaco is still in the catalogue and the blue-dial reference is the one most people picture. Few product placements have aged this well, mostly because it did not feel like one.
