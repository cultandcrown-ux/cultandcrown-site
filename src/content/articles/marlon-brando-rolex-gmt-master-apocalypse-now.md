Some watches are chosen by costume designers. Marlon Brando's Rolex in *Apocalypse Now* was chosen by Brando himself, then modified by him, engraved by him and, decades later, sold for almost two million dollars. It is a stainless steel **Rolex GMT-Master reference 1675** from 1972, and it is the watch on the wrist of Colonel Walter E. Kurtz, one of the most unsettling characters in film history.

This is the story of the **Marlon Brando Rolex**: what it is, why it has no bezel, and how a watch that was supposed to stay off camera ended up in the film anyway.

## The watch Brando was told to take off

Francis Ford Coppola's *Apocalypse Now* was released in August 1979, after a famously chaotic shoot in the Philippines. Brando plays Kurtz, the decorated officer who has disappeared into the Cambodian jungle and built his own kingdom there.

According to the account Brando's family later shared with [Phillips](https://www.phillips.com/article/44804111/phillips-to-offer-marlon-brandos-long-lost-rolex-gmt-master-worn-in-apocalypse-now), Brando wore his own GMT-Master to the set and was told to remove it during filming because it would stand out. His answer has become part of the watch's legend: "If they're looking at my watch, then I'm not doing my job as an actor."

The watch stayed. But Brando did change it.

## Why the Kurtz Rolex has no bezel

Look closely at the **Apocalypse Now watch** and something is missing. The GMT-Master is defined by its rotating 24-hour bezel, usually in red and blue, the famous "Pepsi". On Brando's watch there is no bezel at all: just the bare stepped case around the crystal.

Brando removed it himself. The reasons reported are consistent: the watch looked too polished and too clean for a man who had spent years in the jungle. Without the bright bezel, the GMT-Master turns into something rawer, closer to a tool than to a piece of jewellery. It is a costume decision made by the actor, not by the wardrobe department, and it fits Kurtz perfectly: a man who has stripped away everything decorative.

Brando also left another personal mark. On the caseback he hand-engraved his name, **"M. Brando"**, with an engraving pen. That engraving was never seen by the public until the watch resurfaced in 2019.

## The Rolex GMT-Master 1675, explained

To understand the watch, it helps to know the reference.

The GMT-Master was [born in the 1950s](https://www.officialwatches.com/blogs/articles/the-rolex-gmt-master-through-time-zones-part-1-vintage), when Pan Am asked Rolex for a watch that could show two time zones at once for its transatlantic pilots. The first reference, the 6542, was produced until 1959. Its successor, the **Rolex 1675**, arrived that year and stayed in production until 1980: a run of more than twenty years.

Compared with the 6542, the 1675 grew from 38 to 40 mm and gained crown guards, the shoulders that protect the winding crown. It kept the fourth hand: the red arrow-tipped GMT hand that completes a turn of the dial every 24 hours. Paired with the rotating bezel, it lets the wearer read a second time zone. Without a bezel, as on Brando's watch, the GMT hand keeps turning, but without the 24-hour scale reading a second time zone becomes guesswork.

Brando's example is a steel 1675 made in 1972, with a black dial whose luminous markers have aged to a warm beige. According to Phillips, the dial, hands, case, crystal and crown are all original.

## From the jungle to the family

Brando bought the watch in the early 1970s and kept it for more than twenty years. In 1995 he gave it to his daughter, Petra Brando Fischer, after her graduation from Brown University, together with a handwritten letter. He told her the watch was "like a tank": whatever she did to it, it would keep going.

In 2003 Petra gave it to her husband, Russel Fischer, on their wedding night. He never wore it. For years, collectors assumed the Kurtz watch had simply been lost.

## The $1.95 million auction

The watch reappeared in late 2019, when Phillips announced it as a highlight of **GAME CHANGERS**, its flagship New York watch sale. Paul Boutros, then head of watches for Phillips in the Americas, described it as the holy grail of watches owned by Marlon Brando.

Without its history, a GMT-Master 1675 in this condition would be worth around $20,000, according to Boutros [speaking to Esquire Middle East](https://www.esquireme.com/news/36642-the-multi-million-dollar-watch-marlon-brandos-custom-rolex). On 10 December 2019, Brando's [sold for **$1.95 million**](https://www.foxbusiness.com/money/rolex-worn-by-marlon-brando-fetches-big-money-at-auction). Part of the proceeds went to a foundation set up by Petra and Russel Fischer to help children facing serious hardship.

## Why this watch matters

Most famous screen-worn watches are product placement or prop department choices. Brando's GMT-Master is the opposite: a personal watch, worn on set against the production's wishes and customised by the actor to serve the character. It is one of the clearest examples of an actor using a watch as part of his performance.

It also explains why the bezel-less GMT has become a quiet cult object among collectors. Removing the bezel from a vintage 1675 today would destroy much of its value, but the idea behind it still resonates: a watch reduced to its function, on the wrist of a man who has lost everything else.
