A good watch collection does not need to be expensive. It needs to cover your life. That is the idea behind **The Collection**, our series where we build a **three-watch collection** using only watches that have appeared in films or TV series, with a budget that grows episode after episode.

The rules are always the same:

- **three watches**, each seen on screen;
- **one diver**, **one everyday watch** and **one elegant or unusual piece**;
- a **fixed budget**: for this first episode, everything must fit under **€1,500**.

Here is our first collection.

## 1. The diver: Seiko SKX009, from All Is Lost (2013)

In *All Is Lost*, directed by J.C. Chandor, Robert Redford is the only character on screen: a man alone on a sailboat in the Indian Ocean, trying to survive after his boat is damaged. There is almost no dialogue. What there is, constantly, is water, and on his wrist a **Seiko diver** with a blue and red bezel on a NATO strap.

That watch is widely identified as the **Seiko SKX009**, one of the most loved affordable divers ever made. A small caveat: some sources, such as Jura Watches, point to the **SKX175**, a closely related version for the US market. Both share the same look and the same spirit.

**Why it works in the collection:** the SKX009 is a real diver, not a decorative one: a 42mm steel case, 200m water resistance, a unidirectional bezel to time a dive, an automatic movement and a day-date window. It is the watch you can take on a boat, in the sea or on holiday without thinking twice.

**What to know before buying:** Seiko discontinued the SKX line around 2019, so today it is found only on the second-hand market. Prices have climbed since then, but a good example usually costs **a few hundred euros**. Look for an original bezel and dial, and avoid heavily modified pieces unless that is what you want.

## 2. The everyday watch: Casio CA-50, from Back to the Future (1985)

For everyday wear we chose the opposite of a luxury watch: the **Casio calculator watch** worn by Marty McFly. In the first film he wears the **CA-50**; in Part II and Part III, its successor, the **CA-53W**.

**Why it works in the collection:** it is light, readable and almost indestructible, with a calculator, alarm and stopwatch. It is the watch you put on without thinking, and it carries forty years of film history.

**What to know before buying:** an original CA-50 is only available second hand, while the **CA-53W** is still in production and costs **well under €50**. For this collection, either works. If you want the whole story of Marty's watch, we told it in our article on [the Back to the Future Casio watch](/articles/back-to-the-future-casio-watch-marty-mcfly/).

## 3. The different one: Hamilton Boulton Quartz, from Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny (2023)

The third watch is the one that changes the character of the collection. In *Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny*, Harrison Ford wears the **Hamilton American Classic Boulton Quartz** (ref. H13431553). According to aBlogtoWatch, Hamilton worked with the film's prop master and supplied the watch, which Ford wears throughout the film.

The Boulton is a modern version of a Hamilton design from the 1940s: a **tonneau** case, the barrel shape between a rectangle and an oval, in gold-toned steel, with Arabic numerals and a small seconds sub-dial.

**A detail for watch nerds:** the film is set in 1969, and Gear Patrol points out that a quartz Boulton is not historically accurate for that year. Quartz wristwatches were only just arriving: the first one, the Seiko Astron, went on sale at the very end of 1969. The Boulton fits Indy stylistically, not chronologically, and that is part of the fun of spotting watches on screen.

**Why it works in the collection:** after a diver and a digital, you need something with a different shape and a bit of gold. The Boulton goes with a jacket, a shirt or a pair of chinos, and its quartz movement means you can leave it in a drawer for weeks and pick it up ready to wear.

**What to know before buying:** it is a current model, sold new at about **€795**. It measures 27 by 32mm, so it wears small, like the dress watches of its era, with 30m water resistance: keep it away from the sea, which is the SKX's job.

## Why this collection works

Three watches, three different worlds. The **SKX009** is the tool for water and adventure, the **Casio** is the everyday companion that nobody worries about, and the **Boulton** brings a touch of old-school elegance. Different shapes (round, rectangular, tonneau), different technologies (automatic, digital, quartz) and three very different characters, from a man lost at sea to a teenager from 1985 to the most famous archaeologist in cinema.

All for less than the price of a single entry-level luxury watch. The next episode of The Collection will raise the budget.
