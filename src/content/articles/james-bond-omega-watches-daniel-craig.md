Ask anyone what watch James Bond wears and the answer today is almost always **Omega**. But for Daniel Craig's 007 it was never just one watch. Across five films, from 2006 to 2021, his Bond wore a different **Seamaster** almost every time, and the choice changed with the character: rougher at the start, more classic in the middle, more personal at the end.

This is the complete guide to the **James Bond Omega watches** of the Craig era, film by film.

## Before Craig: how Omega became the Bond watch

For thirty years, Bond's watch was mostly a Rolex, starting with Sean Connery's Submariner in *Dr. No* (1962). That changed in 1995 with *GoldenEye*: Pierce Brosnan wore a blue **Omega Seamaster Professional 300M**, reportedly suggested by costume designer Lindy Hemming. Omega has been on Bond's wrist ever since.

When Craig arrived in 2006, he inherited the brand. What he did with it is the interesting part.

## Casino Royale (2006): the Seamaster 300M and the Planet Ocean

In his first film, Craig's Bond wears two Omegas:

- the **Seamaster Diver 300M** with a blue dial (ref. 2220.80.00), the direct heir of Brosnan's watch, worn with a tuxedo at the poker table;
- the **Seamaster Planet Ocean** (ref. 2900.50.91), a larger diver used mostly in the action scenes.

*Casino Royale* also gave Omega its most famous line in the franchise. On the train, Vesper looks at Bond's wrist and asks: "Rolex?" Bond replies: "Omega." Two words that marked the end of an era.



## Quantum of Solace (2008): one watch, the Planet Ocean 600M

*Quantum of Solace* is the simplest film for watch spotters: Bond wears a single Omega throughout, the **Seamaster Planet Ocean 600M** (ref. 2201.50.00), a 42mm steel diver with a black dial. It suits the film perfectly: a raw, fast revenge story with a tool watch that never leaves his wrist, from the desert to the action sequences.

## Skyfall (2012): Planet Ocean for action, Aqua Terra for the city

In *Skyfall*, Bond starts to wear different watches for different situations:

- the **Seamaster Planet Ocean 600M** (ref. 232.30.42.21.01.001) for the action scenes;
- the **Seamaster Aqua Terra** (ref. 231.10.39.21.03.001), a dressier watch with a teak-pattern dial, for the indoor scenes.

According to Teddy Baldassarre, the Planet Ocean used on set had a titanium case, unlike the steel version sold in stores, and one of the screen-worn pieces later sold at Christie's for more than $250,000.

## Spectre (2015): the Seamaster 300 on a NATO strap

*Spectre* is the film where the watch talks most directly to Bond's past:

- an **Aqua Terra 150M** (ref. 231.10.42.21.03.003) in the Day of the Dead opening in Mexico City;
- the **Seamaster 300** (ref. 233.32.41.21.01.001) on a striped **NATO strap**, a clear tribute to Sean Connery's Submariner on a strap in *Goldfinger* (1964).

As in the classic films, the watch is also a gadget: in the story, the Seamaster 300 hides an explosive charge triggered by turning the bezel.

## No Time to Die (2021): the 007 Edition in titanium

For his last film, Craig did not just wear a watch: he helped design it. The **Seamaster Diver 300M 007 Edition** (ref. 210.90.42.20.01.001), announced in December 2019, was developed with his input. According to WatchesBySJX, Craig wanted a lighter watch that suited a military man, and the details show it:

- a 42mm case and mesh bracelet entirely in **titanium**;
- a brown dial and bezel styled like a vintage **"tropical"** dial;
- a **broad arrow** on the dial and caseback, the mark of British military equipment, a reminder that Bond is a Royal Navy commander.

It is the most personal watch of the Craig era, and a fitting way to close it.

## Why these watches matter

Craig's five films tell a small story through the wrist. His Bond starts with the watch he inherited from Brosnan, moves to the tougher Planet Ocean, learns to switch between action and elegance in *Skyfall*, looks back to Connery in *Spectre*, and ends with a watch built around him. Few actors have shaped Bond's watch as much as Craig did.
