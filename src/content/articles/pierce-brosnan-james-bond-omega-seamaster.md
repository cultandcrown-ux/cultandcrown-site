Before 1995, James Bond had worn Rolex, Seiko and a handful of other brands. Then **Pierce Brosnan** arrived, and with him a blue **Omega Seamaster**. It became one of the most recognisable watches in film history and started a partnership that has lasted more than thirty years.

Here is every Omega Brosnan wore as 007, film by film.

## Why Bond switched to Omega

The choice came from **Lindy Hemming**, the Oscar-winning costume designer of *GoldenEye*. As Monochrome Watches recounts, she was convinced that Commander Bond, a naval man and a diver, would wear a blue-dial Seamaster. It was a character choice before it was a commercial one, and it fits Ian Fleming's Bond, a former officer of the Royal Navy, far better than the luxury Rolex of the early films.

## GoldenEye (1995): Seamaster Professional 300M Quartz, ref. 2541.80

Brosnan's first Bond film is also the first Bond film with an Omega. The watch is the **Seamaster Professional 300M Quartz, reference 2541.80**, with a **midnight blue wave-pattern dial** and a matching bezel.

It is not just a watch: Q's version hides a **laser**, which Bond uses to cut his way out of a train before it explodes, and a **remote detonator**, which comes back several times in the plot. The detail that surprises many collectors is the movement: the GoldenEye watch is a **quartz**.

## Tomorrow Never Dies (1997): Seamaster Professional 300M, ref. 2531.80

From his second film, Bond moves to the automatic version: the **Seamaster Professional 300M Chronometer, reference 2531.80**, powered by **Omega Calibre 1120** with about 44 hours of power reserve. The look is the same, blue wave dial and blue bezel, 41mm steel case, 300m water resistance and the **helium escape valve** at 10 o'clock.

The gadget this time is a **remote detonator**, used against the media baron Elliot Carver.

## The World Is Not Enough (1999): same reference, new gadgets

Bond keeps the **2531.80**, but Q changes the tricks. In *The World Is Not Enough*, the Seamaster contains a **powerful flashlight** and a **miniature grappling hook** with a cable, which Bond uses to escape a missile base.

## Die Another Day (2002): the 20th Bond film

For his last outing as 007, Brosnan wears the **2531.80** again. In *Die Another Day*, the twentieth film of the series, the Seamaster hides a **detonator** and a **laser**. When Q hands it over, he winks at the anniversary with "your 20th, I believe", a line about the watch that is really about the saga.

## What you can learn from this watch: what a helium escape valve does

The small crown at 10 o'clock on Brosnan's Seamaster is not for setting the time. It is a **helium escape valve**, made for professional saturation divers.

These divers live for days in pressurised chambers breathing a gas mix rich in helium. Helium atoms are so small that they slowly get inside the watch case. When the diver decompresses, the gas inside the watch expands faster than it can escape, and it can push the crystal off. The valve lets the helium out in a controlled way.

Bond never needed it, and almost no one who wears a Seamaster does. But it is exactly the kind of professional detail that made the watch believable on the wrist of a naval commander, and the Bond films turned it into the perfect hiding place for a laser.

## Why these watches matter

Brosnan's Seamaster changed two things. It gave Bond a watch that looked like a tool rather than jewellery, and it turned the blue Seamaster 300M into one of the most famous Omega watches ever made. Today the 2541.80 and 2531.80 are sought after on the second-hand market, and modern Seamaster Diver 300M models still carry the wave dial and the helium valve.
