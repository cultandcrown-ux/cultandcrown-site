James Bond has worn Rolex, Seiko and, since 1995, Omega. All great watches, all Swiss or Japanese. But Bond is the most British character in cinema: a Royal Navy commander, working for the Secret Intelligence Service, ordering his drinks in a very particular way. So here is our question for this episode of **Recast**: what if 007 wore a **British** watch?

Our answer is **Christopher Ward**, and we built Bond a complete three-watch collection from its catalogue: one **diver**, one **everyday** watch and one **dress** watch.

## Why Christopher Ward

**Christopher Ward** was founded in England in 2004 by Christopher Ward, Mike France and Peter Ellis. The brand designs its watches in Maidenhead, near London, and makes them in Switzerland; in 2014 it merged with its Swiss manufacturing partner and unveiled its own in-house movement, the SH21. It sells mainly direct to customers, which is why its prices sit well below those of the traditional Swiss brands.

British design, Swiss manufacturing, a career built abroad: it is not a bad description of Bond himself. You can browse the full catalogue on [Christopher Ward's website](https://www.christopherward.com/).

## 1. The diver: C60 Trident Pro 300

Every Bond needs a dive watch. Sean Connery wore a Submariner, Brosnan and Craig wore the Seamaster, and Bond is, after all, a naval officer. Our choice is the **[Christopher Ward C60 Trident Pro 300](https://www.christopherward.com/C60-40ADA31S0KK1-B0.html)**, the brand's best-known diver.

**Why it suits Bond:** it is a real tool, with **300m** water resistance and a **ceramic unidirectional bezel**, and it comes in 38, 40 and 42mm, so it fits any wrist. In blue, it is also a quiet tribute to the blue Seamasters of the Brosnan and Craig years, without copying them.

**Specifications:** according to Hour Striker's 2024 review, the 42mm version measures 11.7mm thick and 49.2mm lug to lug, with an automatic Sellita SW200-1 movement and Super-LumiNova on the hands, markers and bezel. The version in our photo carries "Chronometer" on the dial, a sign of the latest, more accurate generation.

## 2. The everyday watch: C63 Sealander Automatic

Bond spends most of his time travelling, not diving. He needs a watch that works with a suit at the airport, a polo on a boat and a jacket in the mountains. That is exactly the brief of the **[Christopher Ward C63 Sealander](https://www.christopherward.com/int/sealander)**, whose name says it all: at home on sea and land.

**Why it suits Bond:** Ian Fleming's Bond is a man who does not want to be noticed. A black dial, clean steel case and no superfluous details make the Sealander the most discreet watch of the three, the one you wear without thinking about it.

**Specifications:** according to WristReview, the 2026 Sealander Automatic comes in **36mm and 39mm**, with **150m** water resistance and a Sellita **SW200-2 Power+** movement with a **65-hour** power reserve. Prices range from about **€1,125 to €1,390**, depending on version and bracelet. For Bond, we would take the 39mm in black.

## 3. The dress watch: C1 Moonglow

Black tie, a casino, a long night. For Bond's evenings we chose the most theatrical watch in the collection: the **[Christopher Ward C1 Moonglow](https://www.christopherward.com/int/C1-Moonglow/C01-40AMP2-S00K0-B1.html)**.

**Why it suits Bond:** most dress watches are about restraint. The Moonglow is about atmosphere: a black dial full of stars, two oversized moons and a date ring around the edge. It is elegant, but it has a secret, and Bond has always liked gadgets.

**Specifications:** according to Christopher Ward, the Moonglow has a **40.5mm** case, an automatic Sellita SW220-1 movement with the brand's **JJ04** moonphase module and a 38-hour power reserve. The moons and the date ring are treated with Super-LumiNova. On Christopher Ward's US website it is listed at **$3,050**.

## What you can learn from this watch: how a moonphase works

The Moonglow is also a good excuse to understand one of the oldest complications in watchmaking. The Moon takes about **29.5 days** to go from one new moon to the next. A moonphase watch shows where we are in that cycle.

Most moonphases use a disc with two moons printed on it, visible through a small window: the disc turns a little every day, and the moon slowly "fills" and "empties" behind the curved edges of the window. Because 29.5 days do not divide neatly, simple mechanisms drift by about a day every few years and need to be corrected.

The Moonglow takes the same idea and turns it into a show: instead of a small window, the **two moons rotate around the dial**, and the shaded areas reveal the phase. According to Christopher Ward, the JJ04 module is accurate enough to track the real moon for **128 years** before needing a one-day correction. For a spy who often works at night, there are worse ways to tell what the sky looks like.
