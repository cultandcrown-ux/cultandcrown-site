*American Psycho* is a story about surfaces: business cards, suits, restaurants, skincare routines. So it is no surprise that one of the questions fans ask most is about the **Patrick Bateman watch**. The answer is more interesting than it looks, because the watch changes between the book and the film.

**In the novel, Bateman wears a Rolex. In the film, he wears a Seiko that pays homage to one.**

## In the book: a Rolex

Bret Easton Ellis's novel, published in 1991, is famous for its endless lists of brands. Bateman describes what everyone is wearing, from Armani and Ralph Lauren to Valentino, and the watch on every wrist is part of the inventory. According to Time+Tide, **Rolex** is mentioned 26 times in the book, behind only Armani and Ralph Lauren.

For a character like Bateman, a Rolex is not a choice. It is the uniform of a young Wall Street banker in the late 1980s: the most recognisable sign of success you can wear. The book even gives it one of its most quoted lines, when Bateman snaps: *"Don't touch the Rolex."*

## In the film: a Seiko that imitates a Rolex

When Mary Harron adapted the novel in 2000, with Christian Bale in the lead role, the brand disappeared from the dialogue. The famous line became **"Don't touch the watch."**

The watch itself changed too. At first glance it looks exactly like the Rolex from the book: a **two-tone** steel and gold case, a **fluted gold bezel**, a white dial and a **jubilee bracelet**. But it is not a Datejust. Watch publications such as Boss Hunting and Luxury Bazaar, after pausing and zooming in on the film, identify it as a **Seiko 5**, usually referenced as the **SNXJ90**: a much cheaper automatic watch designed to recall the look of the Rolex.

## How to tell it is not a Rolex

The difference is in two small details you can spot in the film:

- **The crown is at 4 o'clock.** A Rolex Datejust has its crown at 3 o'clock. A recessed crown at 4 is a Seiko 5 signature.
- **The day-date window.** Bateman's watch shows the day and the date. The Datejust, as its name says, shows only the date. The model with the day is the Rolex Day-Date, which is only made in precious metals, never in two-tone steel and gold.

Put together, a two-tone watch with a fluted bezel, a jubilee bracelet, a day-date window and a crown at 4 can only be one thing: a Seiko dressed as a Rolex.

## Why not a real Rolex?

The most common explanation is that **Rolex did not want to be associated with the character**. Several publications report that the brand used its influence to keep its name out of the film; Rolex has never confirmed this publicly. It would be consistent with how carefully the brand manages its image in cinema.

Whatever the reason, the result works perfectly for the story. Patrick Bateman is obsessed with appearances and terrified of being second best. Watching him wear a watch that only *looks* like the status symbol he worships is a quiet irony that fits a film where everything is about surface. Look closely, and even his Rolex is an imitation.

## Why this watch matters

The Bateman watch shows how much a single object can say about a character, even when, or especially when, it is not what it seems. It also gave the **Seiko 5 SNXJ90** a second life: today it is often called "the Patrick Bateman watch", and many people buy it for the American Psycho look at a fraction of the price of a Datejust.
